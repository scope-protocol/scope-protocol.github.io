    SCOPE Rail Manual

    A visible payer sends Token-2022 payments with encrypted amounts. For a stealth row, the payer alone builds a fresh confidential account that only the recipient can find and open, in one transaction. The recipient empties it into an account of its own in one more transaction, and the payer gets the rent back.

    After this page you can trace one payment through both transactions, say which party can see which fact, explain the one trick that makes the design possible, and answer the hard questions at a demo.

    SCP-01-00

    What problem does SCOPE solve?

    Context

    Every payout on a public ledger is a permanent record of who got paid and how much. Solana has two partial fixes, and they do not combine. SCOPE is the glue between them.1

    What a chain observer readsPlain SPL transferToken-2022 confidential transfersRFC-42 stealth addressSCOPE stealth row
    Who paidvisiblevisiblevisiblevisible by design
    How muchvisiblehiddenvisiblehidden
    Who receivedvisiblefixed addressfresh addressfresh account
    "Acme pays Dana monthly"readablereadablehiddenhidden
    Statemainnetmainnet, near-zero usedraft, devnet, idle since Junedevnet, first full run 2026-10-13

    Why the two halves do not combine. A confidential account must be configured before it can receive. Token-2022 needs two things for that: the owner's signature, and a proof that someone knows the account's encryption key.3 A payer who pays a fresh stealth address has neither. So without SCOPE, the recipient must come online to set up every single account before it can be paid.

    SCOPE removes that step. The payer configures the account itself, then hands it over. Module SCP-04 shows exactly how.

    Sources: docs/overview.md; verification V1, V15, V46 [1][3].

    SCP-04-00

    The trick: how does a payer set up a stranger's confidential account?

    Mechanism

    The payer is T's owner for the first few instructions of transaction 1. While it is the owner, it can configure T legally. Then it gives ownership away to P, a key only Dana can sign for. This is the handover, and it is the new idea in SCOPE.

    Concrete. In our payment, Acme signs instructions 1-7 as T's owner. Instruction 7 makes P the owner. From that point, Acme cannot move, apply or empty the 980. Token-2022 checks those actions against the live owner.3

    Why it is allowed. Two Token-2022 facts make it work. ConfigureAccount checks only that the current owner signed (V1). SetAuthority(AccountOwner) checks only the current owner, and it clears any delegate (V2). Nothing in Token-2022 says the account must stay with the person who configured it.

    Why each lock exists

    SettingThreat it blocksChecked by
    credit cap = 1Acme keeps the encryption keys forever. The cap lets at most one more secret credit land, so T cannot become a long-term inbox that Acme can read.announce check 11 (error 12)
    public credits offa visible amount sent by Transfer would leak and block the closecheck 12 (error 13)
    close authority = PDAP closing T to itself and keeping Acme's rentcheck 7 (error 8)
    owner ≠ payer, no delegatethe payer, or a delegate it set, moving the funds backchecks 8-9 (errors 9, 10)
    no locked CpiGuarda lock that would make reclaim fail for goodcheck 12b (error 25)
    Note

    A mint authority can still mint a visible "dust" amount into T, because MintTo ignores the public-credit flag (V6). The sweep burns that dust in the same transaction.

    Deep dive: alternatives the team rejected

    Registry path. Token-2022's ConfigureAccountWithRegistry skips the proof and the owner signature, so the payer would never know the keys. But the recipient must be online to create a registry per one-time key. Rejected.

    Pre-published one-time keys. Refills correlate a recipient's accounts and need an always-on service. Rejected.

    Program-owned confidential accounts already exist (Occult, V45). New here is the payer configuring an account for a stranger, then giving it away.

    Sources: docs/protocol.md Transaction 1, "What does each instruction check?", "Which Token-2022 behaviour does this rely on?"; docs/crypto.md Alternatives; V1-V7, V45, V86 [2][3].

    SCP-05-00

    Which keys exist, and who knows each one?

    Mechanism

    Everything per payment grows from one shared secret, S. Acme computes S from its fresh key r and Dana's public scan key. Dana computes the same S from her secret scan key and the public R. One more secret, Dana's spend key, never touches S. That split decides who can do what.

    Concrete. For row 1 of Acme's run, Acme's machine derives r from a run secret (a signature by Acme's own key, so nothing is stored). From r it gets R, which goes on the notice board, and S. From S it gets three independent outputs: the 1-byte view tag, the tweak t that makes P, and ct_ikm, the seed of T's encryption keys.

    DerivationWhy it is built this way
    Two recipient keys (scan, spend)A scanning service can hold b_scan to find payments without the power to spend them.
    R inside every HKDF expansionX25519 maps several encodings of R to the same S. Binding the exact R bytes gives each alias a different t, tag and key.
    ct_ikm binds chain, program, mint and TA repeated R on another cluster or mint does not repeat the encryption keys.
    D_owner from b_spend, R and TAcme cannot derive Dana's destination. T is included so a second account under the same R cannot stall the real sweep (decision 2026-10-09).
    Signing nonce from p only, hand-written signerA nonce from S would let Acme compute p from one signature. The ed25519-dalek hazmat API was involved in RUSTSEC-2022-0093, so the signer is written by hand to RFC 8032.
    Run secret = HKDF(Ed25519 signature of "scope-run-secret/v2" ‖ run id)Deterministic signing lets Acme rebuild every r and every amount opening from its key. Nothing secret sits on disk.
    Warning

    Do not let the payer key sign messages for other software. Its signature over the run-secret message gives every recipient and amount of that run. A leaked payer key exposes all past runs.

    Sources: docs/crypto.md "Derivation tree", "Each derivation and why", "Run secret"; docs/decisions.md 2026-10-09; V25, V41, V53, V61, V75, V85 [4].

    SCP-06-00

    How does Dana find the payment and take it?

    Mechanism

    The notice board has no recipient field. Dana must read every announcement and test each one. The 1-byte view tag makes that cheap: she skips about 255 of 256 notices with one hash. Then one transaction, paid by P, moves the 980 into her own fresh account.

    Scan: a funnel of cheaper to dearer checks

    Sweep: transaction 2

    You might thinkDana needs SOL in some wallet to pay the sweep fee.
    ActuallyP pays. Acme funded P with enough for one sweep at the 1,000,000 micro-lamport price cap plus two rebuilds. If Dana had to top up P from her own wallet, that wallet would link to the payment. The client refuses to send below that floor (V74).
    Caution

    Sweep one payment per session, or use scope sweep --all --jitter-secs 120. Sweeps made close together share an IP, a shape and a fee window, so an observer can group them.

    Sources: docs/recipient.md; docs/protocol.md Transaction 2; docs/privacy.md Limitations; V64-V68, V74, V87 [2][5].

    SCP-07-00

    What does the on-chain program check, and why?

    Decision logic

    The program does not move money. Its job is to make the safety settings of T a property of every announced account, not a promise from the client. announce runs fourteen checks in order; the first failure wins and returns its error code.

    1. 1Data is exactly 33 bytesR (32) + view tag (1)1 InvalidInstructionData
    2. 2-3Payer signed; 4th account is System2 PayerNotSigner · 3 WrongSystemProgram
    3. 4T = create_with_seed(payer, seed(R))binds R and the payer to T5 TokenAccountAddressMismatch
    4. 5-6T is an initialized Token-2022 account6 NotTokenAccount · 7 NotInitialized
    5. 7Close authority is the announcement PDA8 CloseAuthorityMismatch
    6. 8-9Owner is not the payer; no delegatethe handover happened9 OwnerNotHandedOver · 10 DelegateSet
    7. 10-12Confidential extension; credit cap 1; public credits off11 · 12 CreditCapNotOne · 13
    8. 12bNo locked CpiGuard on Tor reclaim could never close T25 CpiGuardEnabled
    9. 13-14Announcement is the canonical PDA ["ann", T] and unused14 AddressMismatch · 15 InUse

    What it does not check: the mint and its approval policy, the amount, and that T's owner is the P Dana will derive. The scanner checks those off chain (V64, V72).

    The decoy attack on reclaim

    reclaim has no signer. Anyone may call it, because the refund can only go to the stored payer. That openness invites a decoy. An attacker makes an empty token account X, points X's close authority at a victim's announcement A, and calls reclaim(A, X).

    Note

    register also checks that B_spend is a prime-order curve point, with the curve25519 syscalls (spike S7). It computes Q = (ℓ−1)·B and accepts only if Q + B is the identity. Every custom error in all four instructions has its own test (V54, V81, V82).

    Sources: docs/protocol.md "What does each instruction check?"; crates/interface/src/error.rs; programs/scope/src/curve.rs; V34, V54, V64, V72, V82, V86 [2].

    SCP-08-00

    Who can see what?

    Privacy model

    SCOPE hides the recipient and the amount from the public. It does not hide the payer, and it does not hide anything from the payer. Pick an observer below to see what they learn about Acme's payment to Dana.

    Observer lensTeaching copy of docs/privacy.md "Who learns what", for one stealth payment.
    FactThis observerWhy
    You might thinkSCOPE is a mixer.
    ActuallyNo pool, no commingling, a visible payer and amounts that either side can disclose. Each payment stands alone. The team's docs say it must not be described as a mixer.
    You might thinkOnce paid through SCOPE, Dana is anonymous for good.
    ActuallyHer privacy ends where she acts. The first public withdrawal, or a spend from D to someone who knows her, links D to her. Merging two payments into one account links them too.
    You might thinkAcme cannot see where its payment went.
    ActuallyAcme sees T, P, D and D_owner after the sweep, and every later counterparty of D (amounts hidden). What stays hidden from Acme is Dana's other income, as long as she never merges accounts.

    Known leaks, in one list

    • Headcount and timing. Every notice stores the payer, so an observer counts a run's stealth rows exactly. The send order is shuffled; the count is not hidden.
    • Fingerprints. D uses the fixed seed scope-sweep/v1, so any D marks its owner as a SCOPE recipient. Calling the program at all marks a wallet as privacy-seeking while usage is low.
    • RPC. Scan, sweep and lookup reveal IPs and candidate accounts to the node. Run your own node or accept this.
    • Mint powers. An auditor key decrypts every amount on the mint. A freeze authority can block the sweep. These belong to the issuer, not to SCOPE.
    • Treasury shielding. Acme's deposit into its confidential source shows that amount once.

    Sources: docs/privacy.md (all of it); V6, V12, V48, V61, V67, V81 [5].

    SCP-09-00

    How does a payout run survive a crash without paying twice?

    Mechanism

    The chain itself is the oracle. A confidential transfer proof is built on the exact current ciphertext of Acme's source account, so at most one attempt per row can ever land. After a crash, the engine reads the source ciphertext and knows which attempt won.

    Concrete. Acme's run is killed after row 1 was sent. On scope pay --resume 3f9c…, the engine reads its journal: row 1 has an open attempt with a signed transaction. It reads the source. If the source shows the post-transfer ciphertext, row 1 landed. If it still shows the pre-transfer ciphertext and the attempt expired, the engine rebuilds row 1. Any other value halts the run.

    GuardWhat it stops
    Duplicate-run guardPaying the same payroll twice. It hashes sorted recipients, amounts, mint and payer, so reordering rows does not slip past it. --again overrides.
    --dry-runSurprises. It checks input, mint, source, recipients and SOL, and samples fees. It sends nothing.
    Mint policyMints SCOPE cannot serve: transfer fee (even 0 bps), transfer hook, non-transferable, paused; and for stealth rows, mints that need issuer approval.
    Cluster pin, --yesResuming against the wrong cluster; sending on mainnet by accident.
    Exit codes1 refused before sending · 3 retry or resume later · 4 stopped after sending.

    Sources: docs/payer.md; docs/decisions.md 2026-10-10; crates/client/src/pay.rs, journal.rs; V48, V83, V84, V90, V91, V92 [6].

    SCP-12-00

    Hard questions, short answers

    Is this a mixer?

    No. There is no pool and no commingling. The payer is visible on every payment, and either side can disclose any amount. A FinCEN 2023 proposal lists single-use addresses as a mixing indicator; no agency has ruled on this shape, and the team plans counsel before any "compliant" claim (Q5).

    Why not just use Token-2022 confidential transfers?

    They hide the amount but keep the recipient's fixed address, so "Acme pays Dana every month" stays public. SCOPE adds a fresh account per payment without Dana being online.

    Why not a pool like Hinkal or Umbra?

    A pool hides the payer too, which SCOPE does not. In exchange, SCOPE has no custody, no anonymity set to fill, and nothing to trust beyond Solana's own programs. The money stays an ordinary Token-2022 token the whole time.

    Can the payer take the money back?

    No. After instruction 7, T's owner is P. Moving, applying or emptying needs P's signature, and p needs Dana's spend key, which never touches the shared secret. The payer can read T's balance, nothing more.

    What if the recipient never sweeps?

    The funds stay safe in T, and the payer's 0.0040 SOL of rent stays locked. Nothing on chain can force the sweep. This is open question Q10 and a stated limitation.

    What does the program do if it never touches funds?

    Three jobs. announce checks the one-time account's safety settings on chain and posts the notice. reclaim returns rent to the payer only. register is an optional address book in the sRFC-42 layout.

    Which token can I use today?

    None of the major stablecoins. PYUSD and USDG carry transfer-fee and hook extensions and need issuer approval for new accounts; USDC is not a Token-2022 mint. The first use is a mint that auto-approves accounts and has neither extension. An issuer path is a one-page spec (Q9).

    Is it audited?

    Not externally. 350 tests pass, including an attack test per program check, a chaos test over 60 kill points, and known-answer vectors from an independent Python generator. The crypto review request (Q1) is the next outside check.

    What does it cost?

    About 0.0086 SOL per stealth row up front, of which 0.0040 comes back. A plain row costs only its fee. See the calculator in SCP-10.

    Who can see the amounts?

    Payer and recipient; the mint's auditor, if the issuer set one; and anyone the payer or recipient gives a receipt. Not the public, not an RPC node.

    Glossary

    Lookup
    payer
    The party that sends payments and signs every transaction of a run. Always visible.
    recipient
    The party that receives. For a stealth row, hidden from the public.
    plain row
    A payment into the recipient's existing confidential account. Amount hidden, address fixed.
    stealth row
    A payment into a fresh one-time account T that only the recipient can find and open.

    b_scan, b_spend
    The recipient's two long-term secrets: one finds payments, one spends them.

    One public byte derived from S. Lets a scanner skip about 255 of 256 notices.
